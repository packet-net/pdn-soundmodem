# HF

At the end of this page your modems are placed on an HF band by frequency, and pdn-soundmodem has told you what to set the dial to.

## Before you start

- A station that already decodes something, from [02-first-station.md](02-first-station.md).
- An HF transceiver with a data mode, wired up as in [03-radios-and-interfaces.md](03-radios-and-interfaces.md). A FlexRadio or a public web receiver works too and tunes itself.
- The frequencies you want to use, written the way a band plan states them: RF, in Hz.

## Sideband and the passband

An SSB transceiver hands you a slice of the band as audio. A rig with an ordinary data filter passes roughly 300 to 2700 Hz, and everything the modem hears or sends lives inside that window. On upper sideband a 1500 Hz tone is the dial plus 1500 Hz on the band. On lower sideband it is the dial minus 1500 Hz. HF data work is upper sideband nearly everywhere, and the top-level `sideband` key defaults to `"usb"`.

One dial serves the whole window, so your modems are not independent of each other. A 2400 Hz window holds several narrow modes side by side, and moving the dial moves every one of them together. Audio close to the edges of the window sits on the filter skirts and loses level.

## Place your modems by RF frequency

Give each modem an `rfFrequency` in Hz in place of an audio `frequency`, and state the sideband once at the top level.

```json
{
  "sideband": "usb",
  "modems": [
    { "subChannel": 0, "mode": "afsk300-il2pc", "rfFrequency": 7050300, "port": 8101 },
    { "subChannel": 1, "mode": "ardop", "rfFrequency": 7050950, "bandwidth": 500, "port": 8200 },
    { "subChannel": 2, "mode": "bpsk300", "rfFrequency": 7051600, "port": 8102 }
  ]
}
```

That is a 40 m plan with three modes in one passband: IL2P over 300 baud AFSK, ARDOP for Winlink, and 300 baud BPSK. The two packet modems get a KISS port each and also answer on the shared KISS port under their sub-channel. ARDOP's `port` is its host interface for Pat or Winlink Express, and it takes the next port up as well; see [06-connect-your-software.md](06-connect-your-software.md).

Restart the service and the journal prints the dial to set:

```
dial: 7.049450 MHz USB - set your radio to this
  modem 0 afsk300-il2pc at 7.050300 MHz = 850 Hz audio
  modem 1 ardop at 7.050950 MHz = 1500 Hz audio
  modem 2 bpsk300 at 7.051600 MHz = 2150 Hz audio
```

Set the radio to 7.049450 MHz USB. The three modems then sit at the RF frequencies you asked for. The dial is chosen to centre the whole group in the passband, which is why it lands on an odd-looking number. The round 7.050000 would leave the AFSK modem occupying 136 to 464 Hz, half of it on the filter skirt.

Four rules apply to a plan written this way.

- Every modem carries an `rfFrequency`, or none does. The dial is shared, so a modem left on an audio centre would land at whatever RF the others' dial put it on.
- One modem cannot have both `frequency` and `rfFrequency`. They say the same thing two ways.
- `fsk9600` and the `c4fsk*` family occupy the audio band from DC upwards and have no centre to place, so they cannot share an SSB band plan. Give them a radio of their own, or an FM radio (below).
- Widths are measured off each modem, so the plan reserves the room the modes occupy. ARDOP negotiates its width per session, so it is planned at 2000 Hz unless `bandwidth` says otherwise. Only 200, 500, 1000 and 2000 are accepted there, and the figure also becomes the widest session the station will accept or ask for.

Every key is listed under [band placement](reference/config.md#band-placement-sideband-dialfrequency-and-rffrequency).

## Pin the dial when you have to

For a net frequency, or to match another application, set `dialFrequency` and it is used as given.

```json
{ "sideband": "usb", "dialFrequency": 7050000 }
```

A pinned dial that puts a modem outside the 300 to 2700 Hz window warns and starts, because that figure is nominal and pdn-soundmodem cannot see your rig's filter. The warning names each modem that falls outside:

```
band plan: WARNING - with the dial pinned to 7.050000 MHz USB, these fall outside the nominal 300-2700 Hz passband. That is only a nominal figure - if your rig passes them, ignore this; omit "dialFrequency" and the dial will be chosen to fit them:
  modem 0 (afsk300-il2pc) at 7.050300 MHz would sit at 300 Hz audio, occupying 136-464 Hz
```

A web receiver has no dial of its own to read, so a `ubersdr:` station needs either an `rfFrequency` on every modem or a `dialFrequency`, and is tuned there for you. See [09-web-receivers.md](09-web-receivers.md).

## Answering a station on its own frequency

Most HF packet stations are a few Hz off the channel. Every decoded frame carries how far off its sender was, and pdn-soundmodem keeps a short window of those figures per callsign.

Once three recent frames from a callsign agree within 20 Hz, a transmission addressed to that callsign is shifted by the offset measured, up to 50 Hz. A station heard 4 Hz high is listening 4 Hz high, so a signal placed there lands on the audio centre its modem expects. Beacons, IDs and other broadcast destinations are never shifted, because a broadcast has no one correspondent to aim at.

A shifted transmission is marked where frames are listed. The journal line carries it:

```
tx[2] bpsk300 GB7RDG-2>EI0RSI-1 37 bytes  shifted +2.3 Hz to suit them
```

The station page shows a `SHIFTED` badge beside the callsigns and `shifted +2.3 Hz` in the detail line, and the frame log keeps the figure in a `tx_trim_hz` column. The badge is hidden while the page is set to FM.

Every shift is clamped to 50 Hz. If that station's own frequency then moves by more than 10 Hz, the correction for it stops for 30 minutes:

```
frequency matching: backing off EI0RSI-1 for 30 min (move 1) - was heard at +4.5 Hz, we began answering +4.5 Hz off to suit it, and it has since moved to +16.2 Hz. ...
```

After three moves the line reads `frequency matching: giving up on EI0RSI-1 - ...` instead and pdn-soundmodem stops correcting for that station. Measurement runs whether or not the transmitter follows it. To keep the transmitter still, set `"frequencyMatching": { "enabled": false }`.

The thresholds are all settable; they are listed under [`frequencyMatching`](reference/config.md#frequencymatching), and the reasoning is in [frequency matching](dev/frequency-matching.md).

## Idents beside the PSK modes

A NinoTNC idents in 300 baud AFSK 200 Hz above its PSK carrier, and pdn-soundmodem listens there by default; [05-modes.md](05-modes.md#ninotnc-ident-beacons) covers that listener and how to turn it off. For your own Morse identification, add an `identify` block with your `callsign` to a modem; the keys are under [`modems[].identify`](reference/config.md#modemsidentify). On a band-planned station you can place its tone by RF with `identify.rfFrequency` instead of `identify.toneHz`.

## On an FM radio

An FM set is a channel radio and none of the arithmetic above applies to it. A 1700 Hz tone out of an FM demodulator is 1700 Hz of audio on whatever channel the radio is on, so `"sideband": "fm"` turns the placement off: `rfFrequency` states the channel, every modem has to name the same channel, and a baseband mode can take one. The start-up report says the channel and does no sums beside it:

```
channel: 145.300000 MHz FM - set your radio to this
  modem 0 c4fsk9600 on the channel
  on FM the audio is audio: a tone is not an offset from the channel, so no modem here is placed by RF
```

## Check it worked

- The journal shows one `dial:` line and one line per modem, with the RF frequencies you asked for.
- The radio is on that dial in a data mode on the sideband you configured.
- The station page header shows the same dial, and the ruler carries an RF scale as well as an audio one. See [07-station-page.md](07-station-page.md).
- A frame decodes with a callsign in it, on the sub-channel you expect.

## If it did not

- `band plan: these modems span N Hz of RF (... to ...), which is more than the 2400 Hz a single SSB passband can carry`, and the service stops with exit status 2. Your modems are spread wider than one radio can hear at once. Move them closer together, or give some of them a second radio. A headless FlexRadio opens its filters as far as 10 kHz before refusing, and prints a `passband:` line when it did.
- `band plan: modem N (X) is a baseband mode`. `fsk9600` and `c4fsk*` cannot sit in an SSB band plan. Give that modem a radio of its own, or an FM radio.
- The dial is right and nothing decodes. Check the receive level first, in [04-levels.md](04-levels.md), then work through [12-troubleshooting.md](12-troubleshooting.md).
- Frames decode but nobody answers you. Your transmit audio is the usual cause on HF, where ALC compresses an overdriven signal into something unreadable; set it with the two-tone test in [04-levels.md](04-levels.md).
