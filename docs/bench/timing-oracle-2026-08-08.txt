rx-roadmap workstream 4, the symbol-timing ceiling probe (2026-08-08)
=====================================================================

Question: how much of the receiver's loss belongs to the symbol clock, and therefore how much
could a second, non-causal pass over a buffered burst ever give back? The workstream claimed
"the remaining ~0.5 dB of DPLL timing loss".

Instrument: sm-ota oracle (tools/Packet.SoundModem.Ota/TimingOracleProbe.cs) over the bench seam
BpskDemodulator.SymbolInstantObserver / SymbolInstantSchedule. The Watterson rig draws its fades
from the burst seed BEFORE its noise, so the same seed at infinite SNR replays the identical
channel realisation with the noise left off. A pass over that noise-free copy records the input-
sample index of every symbol instant; the measured arms decode the NOISY copy reading at those
instants. The DPLL keeps running in every arm (transitions, PacketDcd, and so the deframer's
gating are held constant), so the arms differ in one variable: when each symbol is read.

Arms
  causal   the deployed receiver, recovering timing from the noisy audio
  oracle   symbols read at the noise-free pass's own instants
  grid     a perfect constant-rate clock at the noise-free phase - the transmitted symbol grid
           itself (the sim shares one clock end to end), i.e. the best a perfectly smoothed
           non-causal estimate could ever be
  control  the oracle schedule slipped half a symbol: MUST collapse, or no verdict may be read

Configuration: bpsk300, SINGLE branch (not the deployed 9-branch diversity bank - a bank's
branches recover their own clocks and the winner is chosen by content dedupe, which would blur
what is being measured). Frame 60 bytes, N=200/rung, first seed 1, 3 kHz SNR convention.

Reading: perfect symbol timing is worth nothing. At the AWGN knees the perfect clock buys +3 and
+6 frames against a local slope of ~70 frames/dB - under 0.1 dB, against a claimed ~0.5. Good is
parity. On Moderate the perfect FIXED clock is consistently worse, and the perfect INSTANTANEOUS
clock (the oracle arm) is worse nearly everywhere: -11 to -21 on Good, -3 to -17 on Moderate.

Mechanism, two parts. At 40 samples per symbol behind an RRC matched filter the timing-error
slope is gentle, so the DPLL's residual jitter costs a fraction of a dB and the measurement puts
that fraction under a tenth. And on a fading channel there are TWO candidate true clocks - the
transmitted symbol grid, and the composite channel's instantaneous group delay as the paths swap
dominance - so sampling perfectly at either loses to the DPLL's inertia-limited compromise
between them. The inertia is the smoothing a second pass would be trying to discover.

Hindsight arm, and the lead it leaves behind

  --phase-sweep decodes each burst at every one of the 40 phases and counts it if ANY delivers.
  This is not a bound on estimation (the winning phase is picked knowing which one decoded); it
  measures how much DECODING DIVERSITY sits across the sampling phase.

    Awgn  -5 dB, N=100: causal 34, any-phase 70
    Awgn  -4 dB, N=100: causal 69, any-phase 95
    Good  +0 dB, N=100: causal 57, any-phase 64
    Good  +3 dB, N=100: causal 71, any-phase 76
    Mod   +0 dB, N=100: causal 34, any-phase 47
    Mod   +3 dB, N=100: causal 52, any-phase 61

  A receiver CAN select on that criterion - a frame passes IL2P RS+CRC or it does not - so this
  is a real architecture (timing diversity, the frequency bank's trick along the other axis) and
  not an oracle. But it must be sized against the DEPLOYED bank, not against one branch: the
  9-branch frequency bank on the same seeds scores 56/100 at -5 dB, 88/100 at -4 and 95/100 at
  -3, so it already banks most of this diversity, leaving at most ~14 and ~7 points of headroom
  at those rungs before any overlap is subtracted. Against that, each added decoder multiplies
  the bank's false-accept exposure. Measure phase-union-on-top-of-the-bank before building.

Instrument fault found and fixed en route (the campaign's standing lesson, again)

  The first displacement profile read exactly 0/100 for every negative displacement and a plain
  curve for the positive ones. That was not the receiver: schedule entries falling before the
  first sample could never equal the sample counter, so the schedule stalled at its first entry
  and emitted nothing - a stall that reads exactly like "this timing does not decode". The
  matcher now steps over instants already passed, and TimingOracleSeamTests pins it. Every
  headline number above was re-measured after the fix (the main arms were never affected: their
  instants are non-negative).
